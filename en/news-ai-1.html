Ventilation of a business center in Astana: project implementation experience and engineering solutions

Analysis of a practical case study on the design and installation of a ventilation system for a business center in Astana. Climate specifics, selection of HVAC equipment, energy efficiency requirements, and the experience of TopVentus company in Kazakhstan.

Astana is a city where architectural ambitions collide with harsh climatic realities. Temperature fluctuations from -40°C in winter to +35°C in summer, strong winds, and dust storms impose heightened demands on building life-support systems. Ventilation of a business center under such conditions is not just a matter of comfort, but a question of safety and continuity of business processes.

In this article, we will review the practical experience of implementing a ventilation project for a Class B+ administrative and business complex in Astana. We will examine key engineering solutions, typical mistakes, and equipment selection specifics for the Central Asian region, using the work of TopVentus as an example.

Specifics of ventilation design for a business center in Astana

Designing ventilation for a business center in Astana requires a different approach than in Moscow or Almaty. The main factor is the sharply continental climate with a long heating period (about 210 days). This leaves its mark on the selection of the air exchange scheme and heat recovery.

Main challenges in project implementation:

  • Low winter temperatures:Classic systems with heat recovery can freeze. Installation of glycol antifreeze systems or higher-capacity heaters is required.
  • Pressure differentials:High-rise buildings in steppe zone conditions require complex aerodynamic calculations to prevent draft reversal.
  • High density placement:Open-space offices require variable air volume (VAV systems) to adapt to changing loads.

In the considered business center project with a total area of 12,500 m², a hybrid scheme was applied: central supply air handling units with high-efficiency heat recovery (up to 75%) for common areas and compact duct fans for individual meeting rooms and executive offices.

Energy efficiency and heat recovery: project economics

With rising electricity and heating costs in Astana, the issue of energy efficiency has come to the forefront. In the implemented business center project, we applied a system with a cross-flow heat recovery unit. However, calculations showed that for buildings above 9 floors and at temperatures below -25°C, an additional component is required.

Engineering solution: cascade utilization scheme.

  1. First stage:Plate heat exchanger (recovers up to 60% of heat).
  2. Second stage:Glycol recovery loop for preheating supply air without risk of freezing.

This scheme made it possible to reduce heat consumption for ventilation by 42% compared to traditional systems with an electric air heater. The payback period for the additional capital costs was 2.3 heating seasons. For comparison: the use of standard recuperators without a glycol circuit in Astana's climate leads to system downtime of at least 2 weeks per winter due to freezing.

It is also worth noting the use of variable frequency drives on the fans. This made it possible to smoothly regulate performance at night and on weekends, when the load on the business center is minimal. Electricity savings in this case reach 35%.

Installation and commissioning: specifics of implementation in Astana conditions

Installation of ventilation systems in Astana has its own specifics related to logistics and the qualifications of local crews. Our experience shows that the key success factor is supervisory installation oversight by the manufacturer and detailed design documentation.

Practical recommendations for implementation:

  • Logistics:Plan equipment delivery with a 2-3 week buffer. Customs procedures at the Kazakhstan border can take up to 10 days.
  • Project adaptation:Standard designs developed for the European part of Russia require adjustments regarding the thickness of duct insulation (50 mm is recommended for transit sections) and the slope of drain pipelines.
  • Automation:For facilities in Astana, the installation of CO2 sensors in meeting rooms and assembly halls is mandatory. This is a requirement of local energy efficiency regulations.

Important: all TopVentus equipment shipped to Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan, and Tajikistan undergoes additional preservation of bearings and electric motors to protect against condensation during transportation.
